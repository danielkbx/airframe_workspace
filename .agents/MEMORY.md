- iOS/iPadOS analytical surfaces use one consistent gesture contract: Timeline keeps its established one-finger scrub; Graph always keeps its persistent Current Position/playhead line centered, accepts a single tap to set that position, deliberately ignores one-finger drags, and moves the graph beneath the playhead only with a two-finger pan. Graph Pinch changes zoom without changing Current Position, and double-tap toggles between a zoomed-out window equal to the actual log duration and a five-second window centered on the playhead; zoom-out must not add extra duration merely to place the whole log symmetrically around an off-center playhead. Spectrum, heatmaps, Step Response, Frequency Response, and spectrograms use a transient one-finger crosshair after a native 120 ms press threshold, disappear on lift or multi-touch, and retain two-finger pan where supported and Pinch zoom; the native double-tap recognizer takes precedence over crosshair inspection so its first tap never flashes a crosshair. Spectrum and heatmap double-tap toggles between the full frequency band and a 150 Hz window centered on the tapped frequency, clamped at band edges; Step Response double-tap resets its time zoom. On Spectrum and Step Response, native Pinch and two-finger Pan are mutually exclusive for each touch sequence and calculate from one stable gesture-start window; independent SwiftUI and native recognizers must never write the same viewport concurrently. The one-shot ruler mode temporarily owns its single range-selection drag, then disarms and returns normal interaction; its completed visual selection is hit-test-passive and clears through the inspector action or replacement measurement. macOS remains unchanged.
- Spectrum frequency-line downsampling uses source-bin-anchored buckets for a fixed zoom span. Panning may change the two partially visible edge buckets, but it must not repartition interior FFT bins relative to the viewport because that makes narrow peaks flutter in and out during movement.
- Every analytical graph derives its Y-domain once from the complete immutable render model; Pan and Zoom may change only the X-domain/projection and must never rescale Y from the current viewport. Spectrum derives its stable domain from a fixed 2,048-point, source-bin-anchored peak envelope over every complete trace, matching the graph's represented maxima without letting isolated raw FFT nulls drive the scale. It keeps the normal −50...30 dB range when sufficient and extends either bound outward to the next 10 dB step beyond that envelope. Bins exactly at `AnalysisSpectrum.decibelFloor` (−120 dB) are excluded. A fixed three-percent visual inset provides breathing room without clipping or changing represented peak values. Traces, grids, guides, labels, crosshairs, and accessibility projections share that stable domain. GraphMode section scales, heatmap VS ranges, Step Response ranges, and Frequency Response pane ranges are likewise model-fixed.
- **Stable Y-axis invariant:** Once an analytical render model exists, its Y-axis is immutable. No viewport-dependent operation may change it. This applies without exception to every graph, every platform, and every interaction mode.
- Time-domain measurements compute exact display-scaled per-series statistics and conservative per-series oscillation estimates. Stable frequency requires alternating, vertically distinguishable extrema and consistent same-phase periods; selections spanning multiple periods report the mean period and the larger completed maximum/minimum interval count. Frequency-domain selections never derive a global reciprocal-duration value.
- Oscillation extrema use a prominence gate before the final display-precision separation and period-stability gates. The gate is the larger of `2 × display quantum` and 5% of the selected series span, so small raw-sample reversals riding on a dominant waveform do not fragment it without introducing field- or unit-specific noise thresholds. Graph-backed analysis makes two bounded chunkwise passes (span, then extrema); prepared curves use the same rule. Neighboring samples outside the selection establish boundary direction without contributing statistics or periods.
- The Measurement inspector is deliberately compact: time/frequency endpoints appear as one range row; frequency bandwidth and center share one row; each series combines first/last/change into `Values`, minimum/maximum into `Value Range`, and frequency/period into `Oscillation`. Because selection is pane-bound, series headings omit the redundant graph/pane name and use the trace's existing semantic color as a leading dot. Raw detected-extrema counts, completed-period counts, and oscillation-rejection messages are not user-facing; a detected stable oscillation adds its value row, otherwise no oscillation row appears. Every metric label has localized native help plus an accessibility hint explaining the value.
- Range-selection pointer updates are render-local: the drag draft must never enter the environment-owned measurement state or invalidate static chart canvases. The interaction leaf deduplicates and coalesces native events to at most 120 visible updates per second, receives precomputed domain metadata, formats its chip once with a retained formatter, and draws only the bounded band. Surface calculation tasks live behind stable non-observable owners; only committed selections and published results drive the wider UI.

- Reproducible processed data belongs in the OS cache directory, never in Airframe documents. Persistent datasets use independent static format versions and are discarded on version mismatch, corruption, explicit clearing, quota eviction, or OS purging; no cache migration is performed.
- Persistent cache quota is a device-local user preference in exact decimal 100 MB units from 0 through 10 GB. Fresh defaults are 2 GB on iOS/iPadOS and 5 GB on macOS. Valid stored choices, including zero, never adapt to device capacity or app updates.
- Persistent writes must be coalesced and cancellable per document/dataset. A fire-and-forget task per viewport result is unsafe when each task captures a complete value snapshot and all writes serialize through one actor: the backlog can compete with playback and retain hundreds of megabytes after document close.
- Every new data-backed view, analysis, cache, prefetcher, or persistent-cache adapter must declare process-global/document/view ownership before implementation. Anything retaining log identity, bytes, decoded state, workspaces/results, render models, adapters, or tasks capturing them is document-scoped by default. It requires idempotent terminal shutdown, late-work rejection, cancellation propagation, explicit RAM/root clearing, focused lifecycle tests, and repeated live open/use/close acceptance.
- Memory pressure and LRU budgets limit a live document; they do not release a closed one. SwiftUI disappearance is not the authoritative boundary, platform wrappers may legitimately outlive their visible content, and a conventional `leaks` result of zero does not exclude a reachable whole-document retention. Verify explicit shutdown state and live roots.
- A process-global disk-cache store/catalog remaining after close is correct. It becomes a lifecycle bug only when it retains per-document adapters, captured value snapshots, tasks, windows/views, decoded results, or continues document writes after close.
- `LogViewCommandBroker` treats `NSWindow.willCloseNotification` as a permanent close boundary for that live window object, not merely as removal of its current entry. All registration setters reject later SwiftUI callbacks through a weak, pointer-personality `NSHashTable`, so the barrier neither retains windows nor poisons a newly allocated window at a reused address. Depending only on removal or `onDisappear` is unsafe because a retained menu action closure captures `DocumentView`, its workspace, source bytes, and all analysis state, leaking one complete document per close.
- Sidebar log selection retains the native `List` focus on macOS. `LogDataView` must not reclaim focus when `LogContext.stateKey` or the selected log view changes; window-scoped menu key equivalents continue to route through `LogViewCommandBroker` without changing the first responder. Because native `List` consumes unmodified Space before the menu shortcut, the focused log list handles Space locally and dispatches the same broker-backed playback command. Initial document appearance may still focus the detail content.
- A successful platform-document close is the authoritative heavyweight-state release boundary on both macOS and iOS. Explicitly detach the hosted SwiftUI root and clear `AirframeDocumentOpenModel` decoded state, reference logs, `AirframeWorkspaceController.document`, platform snapshots, and `AirframeDocumentPersistence`; never depend on AppKit/UIKit promptly deallocating a closed document wrapper.
- On macOS, clearing only `NSWindow.contentViewController` is insufficient: AppKit can retain the old `NSHostingView` through `firstResponder` and internal view links after close. Replace the type-erased hosting root with `EmptyView`, clear first/initial responder, content view/controller and toolbar, then sever the window-controller link. Otherwise each closed window can retain its reference-log open models and decoded source buffers.
- AppKit posts the real window-close lifecycle before Airframe's asynchronous `NSDocument.close()` persistence finishes. The window subclass must therefore detach its SwiftUI root synchronously in `NSWindow.close`; a teardown performed only by the later document completion is too late and can be skipped if the document owner disappears. The asynchronous close task retains the document strongly until flush, maintenance, and final teardown complete.
- A per-document activity counter is also a task-lifetime owner. Any detached worker it starts must be registered, canceled by document shutdown, and canceled when its awaiting caller is canceled. CPU-heavy synchronous Reader and Analysis loops must check cooperative cancellation periodically; canceling only the SwiftUI waiter leaves source buffers retained and analysis running after close.
- Initial log restoration must begin with no transient selection. Preselecting the first visible log before the environment-backed document state is available makes the initial visibility pass treat that fallback as valid and skip the persisted selected-log key.
- Package-backed `DocumentStateStore.selectedLogStorageKey` must be edited independently of the last published `packageState` snapshot. Mutating `packageState.selectedLog` before `notifyPackageChange()` makes old and exported state equal, suppressing the workspace revision and losing the selection on reopen.

## Airframe Regular-File Container

- `.airframe` moves from a directory package to an uncompressed append-only regular file optimized for streaming and random range reads.
- Physical `containerVersion = 1` is independent from logical metadata `formatVersion = 2`.
- Autosaves append immutable blobs and a complete snapshot commit. After a commit actually writes bytes, the store evaluates full live-container size and may compact through the existing validated replacement path; no-op saves and unchanged close never start full compaction.
- APFS `clonefile` is the tail-compaction fast path; bounded streaming is the portable and cross-volume fallback.
- Legacy packages are not opened as normal documents. They remain byte-unchanged and can only be converted through the explicit migration workflow.
- Duplicate-only FC imports are no-ops. Destructive source cleanup starts only after a durable flush.
- Unknown unreferenced legacy regular files survive migration as opaque blobs and remain live through later saves.
- Raw export writes primary payloads and `metadata.json`, excluding derived caches.
- Container logging never includes payloads, metadata JSON, notes, names, paths, hashes, configuration text, or device identifiers.
- Document payload limits are symmetric: writes reject payloads above 1 GiB (`AirframeContainerCodec.maximumPayloadByteCount`), and reads use `documentPayloadLimits` with the same 1 GiB `maximumReadLength`. The package default of 64 MiB must never be used for document-owned payloads; asymmetric limits made saved logs unopenable.
- Manifest-to-blob matching claims identical `(kind, byteCount, sha256)` payloads greedily via bucketed lookup (`matchBlobs`); duplicate content at several paths is valid, not ambiguous.
- Unknown blob kinds are read tolerantly: not materialized, retained verbatim through saves. Only document-owned kinds (metadata, log, configuration, index) are validated strictly. Decided before any tester files existed.
- `ContainerStore` holds an exclusive advisory `flock` for its lifetime; a second writer fails typed with `containerInUse`. The lock is re-acquired after compaction replaces the inode and released deterministically in `prepareForClose` — relying on actor `deinit` for release caused reopen failures.
- Locator publication and commit-data syncs use `F_FULLFSYNC` on Darwin (fsync fallback logged). Duplicate locator sequences no longer abort open; they degrade to `LocatorIssue`s and the bounded footer recovery scan still runs. `recoveryWasUsed` is logged as a warning at every open site.
- Mid-session full compaction triggers after a written commit when obsolete bytes exceed both 25% of file size and 10 MiB. This includes metadata-only append history as well as deleted payloads. Existing oversized documents are evaluated on their next commit; unchanged open/close performs no maintenance copy. Close does not independently request compaction, but finishes a previously requested deletion compaction. Thresholds are internal-injectable for tests.
- Full compaction prepares its validated candidate in a same-volume item-replacement workspace because the document security scope may authorize only the exact opened file. Publication still atomically replaces that exact URL and re-acquires the container lock.
- Foundation's JSONDecoder caps nesting at ~512 levels (`DecodingError.dataCorrupted`), so `JSONValue`'s recursive decoding cannot stack-overflow on hostile container metadata; `JSONValueNestingTests` documents this. No depth limit needed.
- Autosave failures surface through `workspace.saveError` in a `DocumentView` banner (`SaveFailureBanner`, retry via `retrySilentSave()`); iOS close-flush failures keep the error pending on the controller. Silent-save state must never be log-only.
- Persisted per-log position and In/Out range use the stable `source:<sha256>:<segmentIndex>` identity, never the segment index alone. Numeric keys remain a read-only legacy fallback and are retained for compatibility; a new mutation writes the stable key, which takes precedence. Transient graph viewports use the same stable identity in memory.

- `MSP` uses the local `Logging` package category `msp.protocol` for direction, version, code, byte counts, retries, failures, and connection lifecycle. It never logs MSP payload bytes or CLI command/response text.
- `FlightController` owns transport-neutral discovery and byte-transport protocols plus the Betaflight handshake. It accepts MSP API 1.44 through 1.48, marks newer API 1.x versions as untested, and rejects non-`BTFL` variants.
- `FlightController` logs handshake stages, lifecycle, and dataflash metadata through `flight-controller.protocol`; it never logs payload bytes or device identifiers.

## User-Facing Text

- Every user-facing label, title, caption, event name, issue message, placeholder, menu item, and accessibility string is owned by `AirframeCaptions` and backed by `.xcstrings`.
- Raw log values, filenames, numeric values, debug-only test names, internal IDs, and machine-readable JSON keys are exceptions.
- User-facing numeric output uses focused locale-aware formatters; units belong in headers, not every numeric cell.
- Public comments explain the format or local design directly and do not cite upstream implementation choices.
- Terminology: the product term is capitalized "Blackbox" in every sentence position; unfiltered gyro signals are labeled "Gyro Unfiltered" (user decision 2026-07-29); PID term labels are hyphenated ("D-Term", "P-Term", "F-Term"); the spectrum window is the "Hann window"; the adjustment event family is spelled "Inflight adjustment".
- Overview Maximum Throttle derives percent from `setpoint[3]` (0...1000, /10) or as fallback `rcCommand[3]` (µs, (v-1000)/10); throttle and altitude extrema never pool multiple source fields. Reader-visible Overview calculation changes must bump `overviewCacheAlgorithmVersion`.

## Documents

- The app registers raw `.bbl` and `.bfl` files plus regular-file `.airframe` documents. `.txt` and `.log` are not system-wide document types.
- Raw logs are read-only and never modified.
- `AirframeWorkspaceDocument` is the shared logical model for raw logs and `.airframe` documents. Physical container details live in `ARCHITECTURE.md`.
- Logical metadata format version 2 contains the ordered log descriptors, flight-controller import snapshots, content-addressed configurations, and document state; logical version 1 remains readable until a real mutation.
- `FlightControllerImportPayload` is independent of document creation. `AirframeImportMaterializer` uses one atomic mutation for new-document creation and append; duplicate-only imports are no-ops, while configuration-only payloads may extend an existing document.
- All embedded sources are equal; there is no persistent main/reference distinction. Source insertion order drives global `Log N` numbering, reorder UI does not exist, and documents have no fixed source limit. Raw windows may hold one main log plus at most eight temporary references.
- Document metadata is authoritative. Raw-log state remains in the external document-state repository and seeds document state once during conversion.
- Document mutations are coalesced and silently persisted. Closing or replacing a workspace flushes pending changes; failed writes remain pending for retry.
- Airframe documents have no persistent document UUID. Runtime windows use ephemeral identity; source/cache identity uses full SHA-256.
- Bookmarks are deliberately absent from format version 1 until behavior and types are designed.
- macOS native Save, Save As, and Revert remain disabled. Duplicate is a coordinated byte-preserving filesystem copy; Rename and Move remain available for Airframe documents.

Wi-Fi and Mass Storage imports use Bluetooth because provenance describes the initial controller contact rather than the later byte-transfer channel. Duplicate bytes do not overwrite the original provenance. Old FC imports without this field remain unknown; they are never guessed as files. The Log Overview card presents the known source with a semantic icon and label.

Raw MSP records remain comments that preserve additional structured configuration for a future Airframe restore mapper. Nyquist status is green at `>= 800 Hz`, yellow at `500..<800 Hz`, and red below `500 Hz`; Spectrum and Overview share the same Reader-owned rate resolver while analysis retains the unsimplified rate.

## Flight Controller Import

- Direct FC import is approved as a staged feature.
- macOS supports direct USB serial and BLE; iOS/iPadOS support BLE only.
- The assistant flow is Prepare, Device, Connect, Content, and Import. It returns a reusable file-backed payload rather than creating a document itself.
- The assistant shell is driven by a testable app-side state machine. macOS device selection prefers USB and otherwise selects the strongest BLE device; iOS selects the strongest BLE device.
- The first release reads onboard FlashFS through MSP and can save a CLI `dump`. MSP SD-card file download is unavailable.
- macOS USB discovery uses IOKit USB serial callout devices and opaque stable IDs. Serial I/O uses `/dev/cu.*` at 115200 8N1 raw mode on a dedicated queue.
- The USB path is hardware-validated with a sandboxed SpeedyBee F405 V5 running Betaflight 4.5.2. Build revision fields are printable seven-byte ASCII and may contain `norevis`, not only Git hex.
- FlashFS download uses uncompressed 4,096-byte MSP v1 jumbo reads, writes validated bytes directly to a temporary file, reports byte-derived progress, and retries a timed-out chunk once. Normal downloads remove partial files on final failure or cancellation; explicit resume mode preserves confirmed partial bytes.
- Configuration download uses framed STX/ETX CLI on Betaflight 4.5.4 and newer. Older firmware uses interactive CLI, recognizes either the batch-end marker or a settled terminal prompt after a verified dump header, then exits and treats the rebooting serial connection as disconnected.
- Complete configuration snapshots require `dump all`; `diff all` is not accepted because effective values would depend on exact firmware defaults. USB and every recognized Bluetooth profile except the constrained shared-`FFE1` `FFE0 UART` layout use `dump all`; unresolved Bluetooth remains conservative. The shared-`FFE1` layout uses comprehensive segmented capture with generated catalogs for BF 4.3, 4.4, 4.5, 2025.12, and 2026.6. Raw MSP records and unavailable fields remain explicit; segmented captures never masquerade as byte-equivalent `dump all` files. Direct constrained capture runs before the log and folds into overall progress; Direct complete-dump capture retains the pre-segmentation order after the log transfer because that is the hardware-proven SpeedyBee path. Wi-Fi and Mass Storage show configuration as their own initial determinate stage without technical request counts. The tested T-Motor shared-`FFE1` profile is constrained; SpeedyBee V2 `ABF0`/`ABF1`/`ABF2` and the other recognized split-characteristic profiles use the fast complete-dump path. Unknown/unresolved capability stays segmented. The following compressed 360,448-byte FlashFS transfer completed in 50 seconds. Keep these figures as evidence for why recognized nonconstrained profiles should avoid segmented capture; continue optimizing the bounded path for shared-`FFE1` hardware.

Direct inspection of the original 8,388,608-byte `/Volumes/STITCH/LOGS/LOG00001.BFL` (SHA-256 `bd6bab73246f94486c2144ecd9185da6217cd12680c39e085bd0ec5a7b55dfc9`) found Blackbox start markers at offsets 0, 979,456, 2,490,368, and 2,785,280. BLE uses Betaflight Huffman-compressed dataflash reads with a 400-byte output budget. A 256-byte budget can produce a valid zero-character compressed response when no complete 256-byte source block fits; 512-byte responses failed after initially transferring data. The 400-byte path ran for more than four minutes and decoded over 837 KiB without retries, timeouts, checksum failures, or disconnection, but throughput remained roughly 3.2 KiB/s. The latter live case had 51 names for 37 definitions and was byte-identical through Direct and Mass Storage. Both recoveries change interpreted metadata only, preserve the original source bytes, and report a warning; do not generalize them into broad header guessing. ## Aircraft and Filter Guides

- Filter Guide visibility is one synchronized Airframe-document Boolean, defaults to enabled, and never has per-log overrides. Raw logs cannot enable or render guides.
- Propeller size has a document default with optional per-log overrides. Rendering requires both document-level visibility and an effective size that supports guides; an unknown size never changes the visibility setting.
- Motor layout follows the same document-default plus per-log-override rule as propeller size. The Overview Aircraft card and the shared Aircraft Settings dialog expose both values, and the effective motor layout feeds the Craft presentation.
- When no motor layout is stored, the Craft timeline's detected template is shared by the Craft presentation, Overview Aircraft card, Aircraft Settings preselection, and CG analysis, then persisted automatically through the normal Aircraft document-state path. The first detected layout becomes the document default; automatic detection never replaces an existing document default or log override.
- The legacy combined `spectrumGuideProfiles` state migrates once into propeller sizes plus document visibility: document `.off` becomes disabled, every other or missing document value becomes enabled, and legacy log `.off` values do not create visibility overrides.
- CG estimation uses robust medians of RPM-squared center-of-thrust samples and deliberately stays silent unless a conventional Quad X, Quad X 1234, or Quad Plus has at least 100 nearly level, low-setpoint samples with complete eRPM and accelerometer-bounded attitude data. Noticeable confident shifts appear as one Checks row; its user-facing detail states only the approximate component directions, overall displacement, confidence, and practical counter-movement guidance, without exposing sample counts or estimator mechanics. Any reliable estimate is rendered as a red top-face sticker in the Craft preview using the same projection and face offset as the forward arrow.
- Successful CG estimates are cached per log storage key in the Airframe document with the effective motor layout and estimator algorithm version. Cache reads require both to match; layout changes or future algorithm-version bumps invalidate old results without deleting them eagerly. Raw logs never persist this cache.

## Persistent Derived-Data Cache Decisions (2026-08-03)

- Persistent derived data belongs only in the OS-provided caches directory, never inside an Airframe document. Each dataset has an independent static code version; incompatible or corrupt entries are discarded and recomputed without migration.
- The device-local quota uses exact decimal 100 MB units over 0...10 GB. Zero disables and clears persistent caching while memory caches remain active. A new metrics subscription must prepare and reconcile the persistent catalog before emitting its initial value, so Settings reports existing disk usage correctly even when opened before any log or cache operation.
- Fresh defaults are fixed at 2 GB on iOS/iPadOS and 5 GB on macOS. Valid stored choices never adapt to free space, device model, launch, or app updates.
